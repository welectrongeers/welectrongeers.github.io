1. What is the difference between an isolator and a circuit breaker?

This is one of the most common opening questions, and it's really a check for whether you understand switching duty versus fault-interruption duty. An isolator provides visible, no-load isolation for safe maintenance access — it cannot make or break load or fault current. A circuit breaker is rated to interrupt both normal load current and fault current, and carries a defined breaking capacity in kA.

Interviewers are often listening for whether you mention "no-load" for the isolator and "breaking capacity" for the breaker — those two phrases signal you understand the core distinction.

3. What standards govern MV switchgear design?

Be ready to name IEC 62271 (the main series for high-voltage switchgear and controlgear) and mention IS 3427 if the company works to Indian standards. Knowing that IEC 62271-100 covers circuit breakers specifically is a good detail to have ready.

What to do before your interview

  • Review the company's product catalog — know their panel types and voltage ranges cold.
  • Be able to sketch a single-line diagram (SLD) from memory for a basic incomer-bus-outgoing feeder setup.
  • Prepare two or three questions to ask them about the role — it signals genuine interest.
